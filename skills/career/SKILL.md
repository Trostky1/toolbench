# Career

**First, tell the user: "Using: Career".**

Written mostly new (no source skill covered this). Borrows the one-question-with-a-guess interview
technique from plan-think and the voice rules from writing-present.

## 0. Ground everything in Ben's real record

Before drafting, read what exists: memory notes in
`C:\Users\bentr\.claude\projects\C--PortolfioCareerAssests\memory\`, the portfolio PDF and Figma
deck, bentrost.site, and any résumé in `C:\PortolfioCareerAssests`. Ben is an industrial design
student (senior capstone: Axiom) with projects including Go Mode, onn. Smart Watch, Flowstate,
TNT Speaker/Tool Roll, an open-source KeyShot Director plugin, and the Rove OS team project.

**Never invent** a job, title, date, metric, tool skill, award or quote. Unknown → ask, or leave
`[confirm: ...]` in the draft. A résumé lie is worse than a gap.

## 1. Start from the target

Every career piece is for a specific reader. Get (or ask for, one at a time with a guess):
- The role and company, and the posting text if there is one (read it; Firecrawl/Agent Reach
  can pull a public posting or company page).
- What that team makes and values (their products, design language, recent work).
- The single strongest match between Ben's work and that role.
Then pick the 2-3 projects that prove that match. Lead with those; drop or shrink the rest.

## 2. Résumé / CV

- One page. Clean, scannable, ATS-readable (real text, standard headings, no text in images, no
  tables for core content). Design flair goes in the portfolio, not the résumé structure.
- Sections: name + links (site, LinkedIn, email) → education (degree, school, expected date) →
  experience → selected projects → skills (tools: SolidWorks/Rhino/KeyShot/Figma etc. only if true).
- Bullets: action verb + what Ben did + concrete result or scale. "Designed and prototyped X,
  tested with n = 9 users, cut Y" beats "Responsible for design".
- Mirror the posting's real keywords where they're honestly true.
- Deliver as PDF (and .docx if an application portal asks). Check it renders.

## 3. Cover letters and outreach

- Cover letter, ≤250 words: why this company specifically (one concrete thing they made or do) →
  the one project that proves fit, with a detail → what he'd bring → short close with the ask.
  No "I am writing to express my interest". No restating the résumé.
- Cold email / LinkedIn DM, ≤120 words: who he is in one line, the specific reason for writing to
  *this* person, one link to the most relevant project, one small clear ask (15-min chat, feedback,
  referral). Subject line names the thing.
- Follow-ups: polite, short, add something new (a new project, a relevant thought), max two.
- **Draft only.** Never send, post or apply on Ben's behalf; he sends.

## 4. Portfolio tailoring

For a target role, recommend: which projects to lead with and in what order, which to cut, what
one line each needs to say for this audience, and whether a role-specific PDF cut is worth making.
Hand visual changes to design-ui and copy edits to writing-present; this skill decides *what* to
show *whom*.

## 5. Interview prep

- Build a question list from the posting: portfolio walkthrough, "tell me about a project that
  failed", process, teamwork/conflict, why this company, tools, a design challenge.
- For each project: a 60-second version and a 5-minute version. Structure answers as situation →
  what he did → result → what he learned. Know the numbers and the trade-offs cold.
- Mock interview on request: ask one question at a time, wait, then give specific feedback on
  clarity, length, evidence and what to cut. Push like a real interviewer would.
- Prepare 3-5 sharp questions for them, based on their actual products.

## 6. LinkedIn

Headline: what he designs + for whom/what kind of work, not just "Student". About: 3 short
paragraphs in his voice, ending with what he's looking for. Featured: the 2-3 best projects. Posts:
process images with a short, specific insight; no hype.

## 7. Strategy and offers

Job search planning goes through a short tracker (role, company, date applied, contact, next step,
status). Salary/offer questions: give ranges with sources (named, dated) and say they're estimates;
help him prepare a negotiation script, but the decision is his.
